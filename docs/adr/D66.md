## D66: al-Futūḥ al-Bāhira as structured text, extracted glyph by glyph (prerequisite for T6.0)
Status: proposed (contributor PR, 2026-11-20); the owner decides. Nothing in the app or the
patch stack depends on it.
Context: T6.0 was deferred (G-140) because the PDF text layer looked unreliable (3.5% false
flags on Ḥafṣ). Investigation: the PDFs are born-digital (Word to Nitro Pro), and the per-glyph
ToUnicode entries are mostly right and in logical order; ordinary extractors reverse each RTL run
character by character, which scrambles every multi-character glyph. The real defects are few
and systematic: ligature glyphs with incomplete ToUnicode, marks drawn in separate runs, and a
Sakkal Majalla seen/sheen split into two glyphs with the letter's Unicode on the connector.
Options:
1. OCR (Tesseract, Kraken, Qari-OCR): loses the colours, weak on full Quranic vocalisation.
2. A vision model reads each page: tends to normalise towards Ḥafṣ, the wrong error for a
   qirāʾāt book; also ~3,000 pages.
3. Glyph-level extraction with proof (chosen), plus visual review only where proof fails.
Decision (`data/pipeline/futuh/`, README there):
- Glyphs, not characters, are reordered; zero-width marks attach to the base Word wrote right
  after them in the content stream, checked against their ink position in the embedded fonts.
- Prose (Sakkal Majalla): every line is shaped again with HarfBuzz using the owner-supplied font
  (`font-sakkal-majalla`, group `owner-fonts`, not redistributable) and its glyph ids compared
  with the PDF's; a mismatch is corrected from the font's cmap/GSUB and checked again.
- Quranic text (old KFGQPC Hafs fonts, not obtainable): per-glyph letter and mark-placement
  corrections are learned from the book's uncoloured text where it follows the Ḥafṣ spine
  (`data/books/al-futuh-al-bahira/glyphfix.json`, with evidence counts; >= 3 consistent
  occurrences). Coloured (variant) words use the same per-glyph rules and are never compared
  letter by letter to Ḥafṣ, so nothing is pulled towards Ḥafṣ (rule 1).
- Every word is anchored to the Ḥafṣ spine and graded against the 8 KFGQPC riwāyāt
  (`hafs-strict/-loose`, `riwayah-strict/-loose` with names, `unattested`, `unaligned`); a
  `decode` field says whether every glyph of the word was confirmed elsewhere.
- Each row's reader label resolves to the 20 ruwat with the book's own terms (terms table v1
  p11, madd schools v1 pp52-53), as bounds (`ruwat_min`/`ruwat_max`) where the book leaves it
  open; nothing is guessed.
- Book text is written only to `build/` (rule 10). Tracked: code, the lexicons
  (`readers.yaml`, `colors.yaml`: names, terms and page citations, no passages) and
  `glyphfix.json` (font glyph data).
Evidence (all six volumes, 2,984 pages, 48,603 table rows, 345,367 Quranic words): every row
anchored to an ayah; 87.3% of words equal the Ḥafṣ spine (82.9% strictly), 7.6% equal another
KFGQPC riwāyah, 4.9% unattested (mostly the 12 riwāyāt KFGQPC does not publish), 0.2% unaligned;
prose runs 93.2% exact round trip, 5.0% equivalent, 1.8% corrected from the font, 22 flagged
(Hijri dates; HarfBuzz does no bidi reordering of digits). A visual calibration of 60 flagged
words drove fixes that took the sample from 42 extraction errors to 6. Reader labels: 88.0%
resolved, 11.0% bounded, 0.8% contextual, 0.2% unresolved (typos in the book).
Consequences: T6.0's comparison can read `build/books/al-futuh-al-bahira/volN.jsonl` instead of
the PDF text layer. Open items are G-219 to G-224. Run: `just book-futuh --vol N`.
