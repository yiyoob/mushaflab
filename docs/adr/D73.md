## D73: The engine takes the Ḥafṣ spine and the layouts from the corpus, through the existing types
Status: proposed
Date: 2026-11-23
Context: M1.4 makes the new model the source of the Ḥafṣ text the app draws, with nothing the
renderer draws allowed to change. The engine (D56) reads a `DataSet` (legacy spine, layouts,
patches) through a `Source`; the seven other riwāyāt still resolve as patches on the spine
`hafs/v1` until M3 (D67).
Options:
1. Teach the resolver, mapper and fitter to read corpus types directly now.
2. Convert the corpus into the engine's current types (`Spine`, `Layout`) when the data is
   loaded, replacing the legacy objects of the same ids; prove the two equal; move the engine onto
   corpus types when M2 needs readings that patches cannot express.
Decision: 2.
- `mushaf_corpus::legacy` converts `corpus/text/words.tsv` into the spine `hafs/v1` (clusters by
  `mushaf_text::clusterize`) and each `corpus/layouts/<id>/` into a `Layout` (a surah header
  takes the surah of the text line after it on its page; a corrected line carries its
  correction's evidence; `layout.yaml` pins are by role, so the layout's sources are the legacy
  ones).
- `Source` gains `corpus_files()`; a checkout reads `corpus/`, the web bundle carries it.
  `mushaf_engine::load_data` is the one loader (CLI renderer, sessions, WASM): legacy data files,
  then the corpus applied, then the data set validated.
- The legacy spine and layout files stay in place until M1.5 removes them from the Ḥafṣ path.
Evidence: `cargo test -p mushaf-corpus --test legacy_equality`: the converted spine equals the
legacy `hafs/v1` item for item (clusters, separators), and the converted layout equals
`uthmani-1441h` line for line, field for field. `just golden` (604 pages) and `just web-parity`:
byte-identical.
Consequences: Patches of the other riwāyāt resolve on the corpus-built spine (equal to the
legacy one). A corpus that fails to load stops the engine. The bundle grows by `corpus/`
(about 5 MB) until M1.5 drops the 23 MB legacy spine from it.
