## D72: Letters with features: what a reading changes and a writing style draws
Status: accepted (Yusuf, GATE M0, 2026-11-23; relayed by Eisa)
Date: 2026-11-23
Context: In the new model (D67) readings change *features* of letters, and a writing style turns
features into codepoints. The feature set must express every reading in scope, and the KFGQPC
Ḥafṣ text must decode and re-encode byte for byte. The codepoints in play are the 42 base and 32
mark/sign classes of `data/pipeline/codepoint_classes.tsv` (every codepoint of the 8 KFGQPC texts).
Options:
1. Phonological features only (vowel, length, assimilation …), derived from the marks. A sign
   whose meaning differs by convention (U+06E1 vs U+0652 as the sign of a vowelless letter) is
   interpreted on decode; encoding needs the convention back.
2. Two levels: (a) **signs**, a lossless, convention-free naming of each codepoint on a letter, in
   the order written; (b) **features**, the reading meaning, derived from signs through a writing
   style's table (`corpus/dabt/<style>.yaml`). Readings change features; the style re-derives
   signs.
Decision: 2.
- **Letter** = one base (a consonant, alef, hamza, tatweel, small waw or yeh, or a carrier) plus
  its signs. Letter ids are `surah:ayah:word:letter`.
- **Signs** (level a), one slot each: `vowel` (fatha, damma, kasra), `tanwin` (fath, damm, kasr),
  `shadda`, `vowelless` (the convention's sign, recorded as which codepoint), `maddah`, `hamza`
  (above, below), `small_letter` (superscript alef, subscript alef, small high yeh or noon, small
  high or low meem, …), `stop` (the waqf signs), `other` (sajdah, rub', small high sīn, the zero
  signs, the stops of imālah or tas-hīl, inverted damma, dot below, …), each with its codepoint and
  its position in the cluster.
- **Features** (level b): what the style table says a sign means, e.g. "not pronounced",
  "imālah", "tas-hīl", "iqlāb", "madd beyond two counts". A sign's meaning is qirāʾāt knowledge
  (rule 1): each row of a style table cites its source (KFGQPC's sign explanation pages for its
  own muṣḥafs, or the books) or is marked `hypothesis`.
- Uṣūl categories (badal, munfaṣil, …) are **not** derived from signs; they live in
  `corpus/usul/` (one reviewed table per category).
- `encode(letters, style)` must give back the exact codepoints `decode` read; an unknown sign
  combination is a hard failure that names the word (M0.5).
Consequences: `core/mushaf-text` implements (a) and the style-table lookup for (b); it builds for
every target. KFGQPC's own conventions become style tables (`kfgqpc-madinah`, `kfgqpc-warsh`, …),
which is where the legacy `encoding` patches collapse.
